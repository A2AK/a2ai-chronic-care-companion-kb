# Pain Care

## Self Care

[See all self care modules »](https://painguide.com/pain-care/self-care/)

Successful pain care does not always need to be costly and involve traveling to multiple appointments. Some aspects of successful pain care can be done in the privacy of your home-if you know what to do. PainGuide provides instruction for using a wide variety of self-management techniques that can be useful to individuals dealing with chronic pain. While self-management techniques may be all that you will need, they can also be used in combination with professionally-delivered treatments.

Damage to a body part and the pain accompanying that damage are actually two separate problems (even though they may seem to go together). The first problem, fixing the damaged body part, often requires professional help (e.g., medications, surgery, etc.). The second problem, pain, is not located at the site of injury. It is something the brain produces to warn of the injury. Sometimes this warning signal lasts well beyond healing or occurs without any injury. These are examples of chronic pain. While there are numerous professional treatments for chronic pain (see the PainGuide section on Professional Treatments), self-management is actually the foundation of successful pain control and can be used on its own or in combination with these other treatments. Since pain is produced in the brain, it can be influenced by whatever else is happening in the brain. Since you are the person with the most influence over the content of your own brain, you can have a great deal of influence over your pain. This section provides resources for pain self-management.

# Professional Care

![a group of healthcare providers](https://painguide.com/img/professional-care-card.jpg)

## [Medications](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications)

There are many medications, both over-the-counter and prescription, that can help to reduce pain. Some of these medications work best with acute pain, while others can help with chronic pain. Each medication has a slightly different mechanism of action in how it reduces pain. For example, some medications act to suppress nociceptive signals from reaching the brain, while others alter how the brain processes pain once the nociceptive signals are there. This section reviews some of the more common classes of pain medications.

## [Therapies](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies)

Sometimes it helps to work on pain with another individual who can guide you in the methods of altering how the brain processes pain. These skills can focus on the mind, the physical body, or both. Some of these skills are similar to self-management approaches (see the PainGuide section on Self-Care) but may include approaches that require professional guidance. This section reviews some of the more common professional therapies for pain management.

## [Devices](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/devices)

There are a number of devices that act to reduce nociceptive input to the brain or that alter how pain is processed and experienced. This section offers descriptions of some of the more commonly used devices for pain management.

## [Procedures](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures)

Sometimes invasive procedures are recommended for pain control. These are highly specialized interventions that can focus both on problems with a specific bodily area and on nociceptive signaling from that area to the brain. This section reviews some of the more commonly used invasive procedures often used in specialized pain clinics.

# Medications

## Medications

[![a generous amount of pills pouring forth out of two bottles](https://painguide.com/img/anti-seizure-card.jpg)\\
**Acetaminophen** \\
\\
This class of medication can help with both acute and chronic forms of pain but also possesses fever-reducing properties.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/acetaminophen/)

[![a generous amount of pills pouring forth out of two bottles](https://painguide.com/img/painguide-image.png)\\
**Anti-anxiety drugs** \\
\\
When the brain is processing both anxiety and pain at the same time, the pain tends to be experienced as being more intense. Reducing anxiety can both reduce the intensity of pain as well as improve mood.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/anti-anxiety-drugs/)

[![six pills on a table](https://painguide.com/img/opioids-card.jpg)\\
**Opioids** \\
\\
While opioids tend to have some benefit for severe acute pain, little evidence supports their benefits when used in chronic pain conditions. They don’t actually “kill” pain, rather they help you not care about it at the cost of many side effects, including addiction and potentially fatal respiratory effects.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/opioids/)

[![a different angle of pills spilling out of a bottle](https://painguide.com/img/antidepressant-card.jpg)\\
**Antidepressants** \\
\\
While this class of medication can be used to treat depression (hence the name), the chemical action of the drug can also help with pain at much lower doses. This class of medication supports the body’s natural ability to dampen the experience of pain. When depression and pain co-exist, the experience of pain tends to be worse. This medication can impact both problems.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/antidepressants/)

[![pills spilling out of a bottle](https://painguide.com/img/benzo-card.jpg)\\
**Gabapentinoids** \\
\\
This class of medication acts on the central nervous system to dampen nociceptive signals going to the brain for processing.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/gabapentinoids/)

[![pills spilling out of a bottle](https://painguide.com/img/misc-pills-for-medication-care.png)\\
**NSAIDS** \\
\\
This class of medication can help with inflammation associated with both acute and chronic forms of pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/nsaids/)

[![a jar of synthetic cannabinoids](https://painguide.com/img/cannab-card.jpg)\\
**Cannabinoids** \\
\\
Some versions of cannabinoids make you high, while others have pain relieving and anti-inflammatory effects. Regulatory considerations have made it difficult to study this class of medication and guidelines and advice on how to cannabinoids have been challenging to produce.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/cannabinoids/)

[![a needle on a table](https://painguide.com/img/muscles-for-misc-card.png)\\
**Muscle Relaxants** \\
\\
Some forms of pain arise due to muscle spasms. This medication can calm some of the nociceptive signaling to the brain caused by spasming muscles.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/medications/muscle-relaxants/)

# Therapies

## Therapies

[![a patient and her therapist talking](https://painguide.com/img/cbt-card.jpg)\\
**Cognitive-behavioral therapy** \\
\\
What you are thinking, the emotions you are feeling, and the things you are doing all influence how pain is processed in the brain. CBT is a therapy that uses thoughts, feelings and behaviors as tools for decreasing pain and improving function.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/cbt)

[![acupuncture needles on a black rock surrounded by flowers](https://painguide.com/img/acupuncture-card.jpg)\\
**Acupuncture** \\
\\
This ancient treatment uses tiny needles to help re-balance the flow of energy through the body as a means of reducing symptoms such as pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/acupuncture)

[![a patient and her therapist talking](https://painguide.com/img/act-card.jpg)\\
**Acceptance & commitment therapy** \\
\\
At times you may need to accept that pain will be along for the ride; but it does not need to completely derail all that you value. ACT, a variant of CBT, focuses heavily on doing the things that are meaningful to you despite the presence of pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/act)

[![a health care professional helping a man use barbells](https://painguide.com/img/pt-card.jpg)\\
**Physical therapy** \\
\\
PT focuses on the physical body as a source of nociception and uses a broad range of techniques to diminish nociception and subsequently pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/pt)

[![a health care professional helping a man use barbells](https://painguide.com/img/movement-card.jpg)\\
**Tai Chi** \\
\\
This ancient treatment uses physical movement to help re-balance the flow of energy through the body as a means of reducing symptoms such as pain and enhancing general health.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/tai-chi)

[![lotus flower](https://painguide.com/img/yoga-card-image.png)\\
**Yoga** \\
\\
This ancient treatment uses physical movement and posture/positioning as a means of reducing symptoms such as pain and to enhance general health\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/yoga)

[![a woman using a occupational therapy device](https://painguide.com/img/ot-card.jpg)\\
**Occupational therapy** \\
\\
Occupational therapy (OT) focuses on improving functional status even though some pain may be persistent. OT uses a broad range of techniques so that you can learn how to do the things you need to do safely even though you may have pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/ot)

[![a group of people doing tai chi](https://painguide.com/img/eaet-card-image.png)\\
**Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy** \\
\\
Emotionally upsetting events may not get fully resolved and as such, they may continue to influence (worsen) the processing of nociception into pain. EAET focuses on resolving past emotionally charged events so that they stop influencing current pain experiences.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/eaet)

[![a woman being treated in a massage chair](https://painguide.com/img/mt-card.jpg)\\
**Massage & spa therapy** \\
\\
Massages and spa treatments can be relaxing and as such offer short-term relief for tension associated with pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/therapies/massage-spa)

# Procedures

## Procedures

[![Etiam porta sem malesuada magna mollis euismod.](https://painguide.com/img/spinal-fusion-card.jpg)\\
**Spinal Infusion Therapy** \\
\\
This is a surgical procedure that implants a battery powered pump into your abdomen which contains medication for treating pain. The medication can then be targeted to very specific sites along the spinal column.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/spinal-fusion)

[![spinal stimulation graphic ](https://painguide.com/img/spinalcord-stimulator.png)\\
**Spinal stimulators** \\
\\
This is a surgical procedure that places electrical stimulating electrodes along your spinal cord to interrupt the transmission of pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/spinal-stims)

[![Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation in Physical Therapy](https://painguide.com/img/spinalcord-injections.png)\\
**Epidural Steroid Injections** \\
\\
Depending upon the location of pain and whether the objective is diagnostic or the relief of pain, there are a number of procedures that involve injecting medication directly into a specific location along the spinal column\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/esi)

[![Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation in Physical Therapy](https://painguide.com/img/faucet-block-image-for-card.png)\\
**Facet Block** \\
\\
This is a diagnostic procedure involving the facet joint or medical branch. This procedure helps to identify where your pain is coming from.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/facet-block)

[![Lumbar spinal section](https://painguide.com/img/lumbar-image-for-card.png)\\
**Lumbar Sympathetic Nerve Block** \\
\\
This is a diagnostic procedure involving the facet joint or medical branch. This procedure helps to identify where your pain is coming from.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/lsnb)

[![Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation in Physical Therapy](https://painguide.com/img/discectomy-image-for-card.png)\\
**Percutaneous Discectomy** \\
\\
This procedure helps to remove parts of a bulging disc\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/percutaneous-discectomy)

[![Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation in Physical Therapy](https://painguide.com/img/nerve-block-image-card.png)\\
**Peripheral Nerve Block** \\
\\
This procedure helps to determine if a specific nerve is involved in causing your pain.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/peripheral-nerve-block)

[![Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation in Physical Therapy](https://painguide.com/img/radiofrequency-ablation-image-for-card.png)\\
**Radiofrequency Ablation** \\
\\
Sometimes the objective is to simply kill, dull, or remove a nerve that is contributing to persistent pain. This method involves heating specific nerves.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/radiofrequency-ablation)

[![Doctors pointing to spine ](https://painguide.com/img/generic-back-pain-image-for-misc-card.png)\\
**Trigger point injections** \\
\\
This procedure uses local anesthetic to temporarily numb and relax muscles\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/tpi)

[![Cras mattis consectetur purus sit amet fermentum](https://painguide.com/img/cryneuroablation-image-for-card.png)\\
**Cryoneuroablation** \\
\\
Sometimes the objective is to simply kill, dull, or remove a nerve that is contributing to persistent pain. This method involves freezing specific nerves.\\
\\
Learn more >](https://painguide.com/pain-care/professional-care/procedures/cryoneuroablation)
